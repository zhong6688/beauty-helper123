# Candidate products that were screened out

Screening rules for the knowledge base (face moisturizers sold in Singapore), applied to the official product page:

1. The main claim is moisturizing or hydrating. Not brightening, dark-spot, acne-treatment or anti-ageing; no retinol, acids or SPF; no medical-style claims.
2. The page names ONE skin type (oily, dry or sensitive), not "all skin types" and not blank.
3. Enough reviews about the product, not mainly free-gift reviews from people who have not used it.
4. A complete ingredient list is available.

| --- | --- |
| LYDIMOON Aloe Vera Gel Moisturizing Cream 40g | Page says "All Skin Type", first claim is for dry and rough skin (not oily); title and text claim acne reduction, dark-spot removal, anti-ageing and treating allergies; 6 of its 10 reviews are from people who got it as a free gift and had not tried it; no ingredient list captured |
| Olay Regenerist Retinol24 Night Face Moisturizer 50g | Anti-ageing claims (fine lines, wrinkles, firming, dark spots); contains retinol, which needs extra precautions (sun exposure, pregnancy, gradual start) that the assistant is not designed to give; no skin type stated on the page; reviews are mostly days-old first impressions |
| L'Oreal Paris Glycolic Bright Glowing Day Cream SPF30 50ml | Brightening and dark-spot claims; contains glycolic acid and SPF (acid and sunscreen precautions); page says "All Types"; no ingredient list captured |

Note for the report: screening rules were defined first and applied the same way to every candidate. Official skin-type wording differs by country for
the same brand (for example Neutrogena Hydro Boost variants are labeled "normal to oily", "normal to dry" and "dry to extra dry" on one national site, while other
sites list the water gel as "all skin types"), so the label must come from the Singapore listing that was actually used.
