Review sentiment classifier (Part 1)

A scikit-learn model (word and character TF-IDF with logistic regression) trained on 121 hand-labeled reviews about products, running here in your browser. It reads whether a review about a product is positive or negative.

Limits: small training set; in testing it missed about a third of negative reviews and did better on products it had seen than on new ones. It is used here to tag knowledge-base reviews that have no hand label.

Evaluation (14 scenario questions)

Each question runs the full pipeline. Automatic checks: the right kind of answer (recommend, ask for more information, decline, no match), a pick that fits the skin type and budget on the product pages, at least one verified review quote, and a doctor/pharmacist notice when a medical condition is mentioned. You can override any row with your own Pass/Fail after reading the answer. Target: 80% pass.

Know the limits

  • Only a handful of everyday moisturizers are covered (see the Products tab; roughly S$5 to S$42 on the day the prices were seen). Anti-ageing, brightening, sunscreen and makeup are out of scope.
  • Reviews are not a random sample: some were copied in page order, others picked by star filter. Counts of positive and negative reviews do not show how good a product is.
  • A sentiment tag says "hand" when the author labeled the review and "model" when the classifier did.
  • "All skin types" on a product page is not treated as a match for any specific skin type.
  • Brand claims (for example "48-hour hydration") are the brand's, not verified.
  • This is general information, not medical advice. See a doctor or pharmacist for skin conditions, and patch test before first use.
